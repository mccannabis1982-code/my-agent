Snake Plant

Sansevieria trifasciata (Dracaena trifasciata)

Light: Bright indirect light is best; tolerates low light and some direct sun.

Water: Let soil dry out completely between waterings. About every 2–3 weeks indoors; less in winter.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten — keep out of reach.

Common problem: Mushy, yellow leaves at the base = overwatering / rot. Fix: Stop watering, let soil dry fully, remove mushy leaves, and repot in dry, fast-draining soil if the base is soft.

2 / 30

Pothos

Epipremnum aureum

Light: Bright indirect light; tolerates low light (leaves may lose variegation).

Water: Water when the top 1–2 inches of soil are dry.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Yellow leaves, often the oldest ones. Fix: Usually overwatering or old age — check that the soil is not staying soggy, trim yellow leaves, and let the top soil dry before watering again.

3 / 30

Monstera

Monstera deliciosa

Light: Bright indirect light. A little gentle morning sun is fine; harsh direct sun scorches leaves.

Water: Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Needs a pot with drainage.

Humidity: Likes moderate to high humidity; average home air is usually OK.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: New leaves come out solid, without splits. Fix: It needs more light — move it closer to a bright window. Splits develop on mature leaves in good light.

4 / 30

ZZ Plant

Zamioculcas zamiifolia

Light: Low to bright indirect light. Tolerates a dim corner.

Water: Let soil dry out completely between waterings. Its thick rhizomes store water — about every 2–3 weeks.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Yellow leaves and soft stems. Fix: Almost always overwatering. Let soil dry fully, remove soft stems, and water less often.

5 / 30

Spider Plant

Chlorophytum comosum

Light: Bright indirect light. Tolerates some direct sun; too little light slows growth.

Water: Keep soil lightly moist; water when the top inch is dry.

Humidity: Average to moderate humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Brown leaf tips. Fix: Often salts / fluoride in tap water or dry air. Flush the soil with plain water occasionally, and try rainwater or water left out overnight.

6 / 30

Peace Lily

Spathiphyllum spp.

Light: Low to bright indirect light. Avoid direct sun, which scorches leaves.

Water: Keep soil evenly moist; water when the top inch is dry. It droops dramatically when thirsty and recovers after watering.

Humidity: Likes moderate to high humidity.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Drooping leaves. Fix: Water thoroughly and let excess drain — it usually perks up within hours. If it droops again in a day or two, it may need a bigger pot.

7 / 30

Fiddle-Leaf Fig

Ficus lyrata

Light: Bright indirect light, ideally near a bright window. Needs more light than most houseplants.

Water: Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Do not let it sit in water.

Humidity: Moderate humidity; average home air is usually OK.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Leaves drop after a move or a change in watering. Fix: It dislikes change — pick one bright spot, keep watering consistent, and give it time. Avoid moving it often.

8 / 30

Heartleaf Philodendron

Philodendron hederaceum

Light: Bright indirect light; tolerates low light.

Water: Water when the top 1–2 inches of soil are dry.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine; it appreciates a little extra.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Long, leggy stems with few leaves. Fix: Give it more light and pinch or prune the stems back — pruning encourages bushier growth.

9 / 30

Aloe Vera

Aloe barbadensis miller

Light: Bright light, including several hours of direct sun if introduced gradually.

Water: Let soil dry out completely between waterings. Water sparingly in winter.

Humidity: Average to dry household air is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Mushy, translucent leaves at the base. Fix: Overwatering — stop watering, let soil dry fully, remove mushy leaves, and use a fast-draining cactus/succulent mix.

10 / 30

Jade Plant

Crassula ovata

Light: Bright light, including direct sun. Needs good light to stay compact.

Water: Let soil dry out completely between waterings. Less in winter.

Humidity: Average to dry household air is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Leaves drop or stems go soft. Fix: Usually overwatering or too little light. Let soil dry fully, move to a brighter spot, and prune soft stems back to firm growth.

11 / 30

Rubber Plant

Ficus elastica

Light: Bright indirect light; tolerates some direct sun.

Water: Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Do not let it sit in water.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten (mild to moderate).

Common problem: Lower leaves drop. Fix: Usually too little light or inconsistent watering. Move to a brighter spot and water on a regular check-the-soil routine.

12 / 30

Calathea

Goeppertia spp. (Calathea)

Light: Bright indirect light. Direct sun fades and scorches the patterned leaves.

Water: Keep soil evenly moist; water when the top inch is dry. Prefers rainwater or distilled water where tap water is hard.

Humidity: Likes high humidity — group plants together or use a humidifier.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Crispy brown leaf edges. Fix: Low humidity or tap-water salts. Raise humidity and switch to rainwater or water left out overnight.

13 / 30

English Ivy

Hedera helix

Light: Bright indirect light; tolerates some direct sun and lower light.

Water: Keep soil lightly moist; water when the top inch is dry.

Humidity: Likes moderate humidity; dry air invites spider mites.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Dry, crispy leaves and fine webbing = spider mites. Fix: Rinse the plant under a shower, raise humidity, and repeat the rinse weekly until the mites are gone.

14 / 30

Boston Fern

Nephrolepis exaltata

Light: Bright indirect light. Avoid direct sun.

Water: Keep soil evenly moist — it does not like drying out. Water when the top inch feels dry.

Humidity: Likes high humidity; misting alone is rarely enough — group plants or use a humidifier.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Fronds turn brown and drop leaflets. Fix: Usually dry air or missed watering. Water consistently, raise humidity, and trim brown fronds at the base.

15 / 30

African Violet

Saintpaulia ionantha

Light: Bright indirect light. An east-facing window is ideal; avoid harsh direct sun.

Water: Keep soil lightly moist; water from the bottom or at soil level. Avoid wetting the leaves.

Humidity: Likes moderate humidity.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: No flowers. Fix: It usually needs more light or is pot-bound / over-fertilized. Move to brighter indirect light and feed lightly only in the growing season.

16 / 30

Chinese Evergreen

Aglaonema spp.

Light: Low to bright indirect light. One of the best plants for a dim room.

Water: Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Do not keep it soggy.

Humidity: Average to moderate humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Yellow lower leaves. Fix: Often overwatering or cold drafts. Let soil dry more between waterings and keep it away from cold windows and air-conditioning vents.

17 / 30

Dracaena

Dracaena marginata (Dragon Tree)

Light: Bright indirect light; tolerates lower light.

Water: Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Sensitive to fluoride in tap water.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Brown leaf tips. Fix: Often fluoride / salts in tap water or dry air. Use rainwater or distilled water, and flush the soil occasionally with plain water.

18 / 30

Peperomia

Peperomia spp.

Light: Bright indirect light. Avoid direct sun, which scorches the leaves.

Water: Water when the top 1–2 inches of soil are dry. Its thick leaves store some water — err on the dry side.

Humidity: Average to moderate humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Leaves drop and stems go mushy. Fix: Overwatering. Let soil dry more between waterings and make sure the pot drains freely.

19 / 30

String of Pearls

Senecio rowleyanus (Curio rowleyanus)

Light: Bright indirect light, with some direct sun on the top of the pot — the crown needs light.

Water: Let soil dry out between waterings; water sparingly in winter.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Pearls shrivel or the top of the plant thins out. Fix: Shriveled pearls usually mean underwatering; a bare crown usually means too little light on top. Water thoroughly when dry and give the crown more light.

20 / 30

Bird of Paradise

Strelitzia nicolai (indoor)

Light: Bright light, including direct sun. Needs the brightest spot you have indoors.

Water: Keep soil lightly moist in the growing season; water when the top 2 inches are dry. Less in winter.

Humidity: Likes moderate to high humidity.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Leaves split or curl. Fix: Splitting is normal for this plant. Curling usually means it is thirsty or the air is very dry — water when the top soil is dry and raise humidity.

21 / 30

Dieffenbachia

Dieffenbachia spp. (Dumb Cane)

Light: Bright indirect light; tolerates low light.

Water: Keep soil lightly moist; water when the top 1–2 inches are dry.

Humidity: Average to moderate humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten — also irritates mouth and skin; wear gloves when pruning.

Common problem: Yellow leaves and a bare lower stem. Fix: Usually overwatering or cold air. Water less, keep it warm, and prune a leggy stem back — new growth will sprout below the cut.

22 / 30

Pilea

Pilea peperomioides (Chinese Money Plant)

Light: Bright indirect light. Rotate the pot weekly so it grows evenly.

Water: Water when the top 1–2 inches of soil are dry.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Leaves curl inward or droop. Fix: Usually underwatering or too much direct sun. Water thoroughly when dry and move it out of harsh sun.

23 / 30

Croton

Codiaeum variegatum

Light: Bright light, including direct sun — bright light keeps the leaf colors strong.

Water: Keep soil evenly moist; water when the top inch is dry. Do not let it dry out completely.

Humidity: Likes moderate to high humidity.

Pet safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if eaten.

Common problem: Leaves drop after a move. Fix: It dislikes change and cold drafts. Pick one bright, warm spot, keep watering steady, and give it a few weeks to settle.

24 / 30

Phalaenopsis Orchid

Phalaenopsis spp. (Moth Orchid)

Light: Bright indirect light. An east-facing window is ideal; avoid direct midday sun.

Water: Water about once a week — soak the bark, then let it drain fully. Never leave it sitting in water. Use room-temperature water.

Humidity: Likes moderate humidity.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Flowers drop and roots look mushy. Fix: Overwatering or water sitting in the crown. Let bark dry between waterings, drain fully, and keep water out of the leaf joints.

25 / 30

Desert Cactus

Cactaceae — desert types (e.g. Mammillaria, Echinocactus)

Light: As much bright light and direct sun as you can give it indoors.

Water: Water only when soil is fully dry, then soak and drain. Very little in winter.

Humidity: Dry household air is fine — it prefers low humidity.

Pet safety: Most desert cacti are not poisonous, but spines can injure pets and people — keep out of reach.

Common problem: Soft, mushy base or leaning / stretching (etiolation). Fix: Mushy = rot from overwatering — stop watering and repot in dry cactus mix if needed. Stretching = too little light — move to a sunnier window gradually.

26 / 30

Hoya

Hoya spp. (Wax Plant)

Light: Bright indirect light; some gentle direct sun helps it flower.

Water: Let the top 2 inches of soil dry between waterings. Its thick leaves store water.

Humidity: Average to moderate humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: No flowers. Fix: It usually needs more light and maturity — give it a bright spot, do not cut off the bare flower spurs (peduncles), and be patient.

27 / 30

Cast Iron Plant

Aspidistra elatior

Light: Low to bright indirect light. One of the toughest plants for a dim room.

Water: Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Tolerates a missed watering.

Humidity: Average household humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Brown leaf tips or spots. Fix: Usually overwatering or too much direct sun. Water less often and move it out of direct sun.

28 / 30

Prayer Plant

Maranta leuconeura

Light: Bright indirect light. Direct sun fades the leaf pattern.

Water: Keep soil evenly moist; water when the top inch is dry. Prefers rainwater or distilled water.

Humidity: Likes high humidity — group plants together or use a humidifier.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Leaves curl and brown edges appear. Fix: Low humidity or dry soil. Water consistently, raise humidity, and switch to rainwater or distilled water if your tap water is hard.

29 / 30

Tradescantia

Tradescantia zebrina (Inchplant)

Light: Bright indirect light keeps the purple and silver stripes strong; low light turns it green and leggy.

Water: Keep soil lightly moist; water when the top inch is dry.

Humidity: Average to moderate humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Can irritate skin and stomach in cats and dogs — keep out of reach.

Common problem: Leggy stems with bare sections. Fix: Give it more light and pinch the stem tips back — pinched stems branch and fill out. Root the cuttings in water for a fuller pot.

Money Tree

Pachira aquatica

Light: Bright indirect light; tolerates some direct morning sun.

Water: Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Do not let it sit in water.

Humidity: Average to moderate humidity is fine.

Pet safety: Generally considered safe for cats and dogs.

Common problem: Yellow leaves and a soft trunk. Fix: Overwatering. Let soil dry more between waterings, make sure the pot drains, and remove any soft or mushy stems.

General care notes (applies to most cards)

  • Check before you water: push a finger 1–2 inches into the soil. Dry? Water. Damp? Wait. Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering.
  • Drainage matters: every pot needs a drainage hole, or a way for excess water to escape. Never leave a plant standing in water.
  • Light: “bright indirect light” means near a window, out of the direct sunbeam. A plant that stretches toward the window or grows leggy usually wants more light.
  • Repotting: repot when roots circle the pot or water runs straight through — usually every 1–2 years, in spring, into a pot one size up.
  • Pet safety: “generally considered safe” and “toxic” are guides based on common references. If a pet eats any plant and seems unwell, call your veterinarian. Keep toxic plants out of reach of pets and small children.
