📚 Lessons — Plain Language

What is a wallet?

A crypto wallet is an app (or device) that holds the keys to your crypto. It doesn't hold the coins themselves — those live on the blockchain. The wallet holds your private key, which is what lets you spend or move them. Coinbase Wallet is a self-custodial wallet: you hold the keys, not a company. That means no company can freeze it — but also no company can recover it if you lose it.

What is a seed phrase?

A seed phrase is a list of 12 or 24 ordinary words (like "apple river mountain…") that is the master backup of your wallet. Anyone who has those words can take everything in the wallet. Write it on paper and store the paper somewhere safe. Never type it into a website, never send it in a message, never screenshot it, never share it with anyone — including anyone claiming to be "support." A real support agent will never ask for it.

What is gas?

Gas is the small fee you pay to make a transaction on a blockchain. On Base (the network Coinbase Wallet Quests use), gas is usually just a few cents, paid in Base ETH. Some quests may need a few cents of Base ETH to complete. If a quest asks you to pay more than a few cents, stop and check whether it's legitimate.

What is KYC?

KYC means "Know Your Customer" — a company verifying who you are, usually with a government ID and your Social Security number. Centralized exchanges (like Coinbase.com, Kraken, or Binance.US) require KYC before you can cash out to dollars. Self-custodial wallets like Coinbase Wallet do not require KYC to use. That's why quests through the wallet are an option while an identity-theft case is open — but cashing out still isn't, until the case is resolved.

🚩 Scam Red Flags

  • Pay to earn: any program that asks you to pay money, buy tokens, or send crypto first in order to "unlock" earnings. Real learning rewards don't work that way.
  • Share your seed phrase: anyone — a website, a DM, a "support agent," a video — asking for your seed phrase or private key. This is always a scam. Support never asks for it.
  • Guaranteed returns: promises like "double your money," "guaranteed 20% a month," or "risk-free profit." There is no guaranteed return in crypto. Anyone promising one is lying.
  • DM "support" asking for wallet access: strangers messaging you on Telegram, Discord, X, or WhatsApp offering help, then asking you to connect your wallet, share your screen, or visit a link. Real support doesn't DM you first.
  • Impersonation: accounts or sites pretending to be Coinbase, Brave, or a famous person, often with a slightly misspelled name or web address. Check the exact spelling before you trust anything.
  • Too good to be true: free crypto giveaways, "send 0.1 ETH and get 0.2 back," celebrity endorsements of a coin. All scams.
