Assumptions — read before you quote

  • Coverage: about 350 sq ft per gallon per coat, the standard figure printed on most interior paint cans. Rough, textured, or porous walls use more paint.
  • Coats: 2 coats is the default. One coat may be enough over the same color in good condition; a dark-to-light change may need a primer coat plus 2 coats.
  • Wall area: 2 × (length + width) × height. Ceiling area (length × width) is added only if you check “Include the ceiling.”
  • Doors and windows: each door deducts 21 sq ft and each window deducts 15 sq ft from the paintable area.
  • Gallons to buy: exact gallons are rounded up to whole gallons, so you do not run short mid-wall. Buy one extra gallon on a large or dark-color job if you want touch-up paint left over.
  • Labor: about 150 sq ft per hour per coat for rolling walls (including cutting in), plus 1 hour for setup and cleanup per room. This is a planning figure — your own speed, wall condition, trim work, and prep will change it.
  • Paint prices: the default $35 / $55 / $75 per gallon and the $40/hour labor rate are starting figures only. Paint prices change — verify current local paint prices at the store before quoting a job.
  • Not included: drywall repair, wallpaper removal, trim/door painting as a separate line, or moving heavy furniture, unless you add them to the proposal yourself.
A note on accuracy: measure twice. A room that is 12 × 10 ft with 8-ft ceilings is about 352 sq ft of wall — one gallon per coat, two gallons for two coats, before doors and windows are deducted. If your numbers look far from that, re-check the measurements before you quote.
